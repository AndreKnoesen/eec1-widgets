What You Will Do

You will record 20 seconds of smartphone accelerometer data for three physical activities — walking, running, and jumping — using a MATLAB script that guides you through every step. The script saves three files. You submit them to Canvas. In the lab the following week, your batch analysis loop will process the entire class dataset.

Total time: ~5 minutes of active recording. The rest of the 30-minute lab is setup, submission, and helping teammates.

Before You Run the Script

On your phone: 1. Install MATLAB Mobile from the App Store or Google Play (free — use your UC Davis MathWorks account) 2. Open the app and sign in with the same account you use for MATLAB Online 3. Connect to Wi-Fi (same network as your laptop — eduroam works) 4. In the app: tap Settings → Connect and pair with your MATLAB Online session

In MATLAB Online: 1. Download the script by pasting this into the Command Window and pressing Enter: matlab websave("L11_Lab_DataCollection.m", "https://aknoesen.github.io/eec1-widgets/L11_Lab_DataCollection.m"); You can also download it directly at: https://aknoesen.github.io/eec1-widgets/L11_Lab_DataCollection.m 2. Make sure your current folder in MATLAB is set to a location you can find (e.g., your MATLAB Drive home folder) — this is where your files will be saved

Running the Script

Type this in the MATLAB Command Window and press Enter:

L11_Lab_DataCollection

The script will guide you through every step. Here is what to expect:

Step 1 — Setup prompt The script asks you to confirm your phone is connected. Check that MATLAB Mobile shows “Connected” before pressing Enter.

Step 2 — Enter your information You will be asked for: - First name - Last name - Lab section (e.g., A01 — use the code shown on the projector)

Enter these exactly as shown. Your files will be named:

walk_firstname_lastname_section.mat
run_firstname_lastname_section.mat
jump_firstname_lastname_section.mat

Step 3 — Three recordings For each activity, the script prints instructions, then waits for you to press Enter. Once you press Enter: - Hold the phone in your hand (portrait orientation, screen facing you) - A 20-second countdown appears: 20 19 18 ... - Perform the activity continuously for the full countdown - The script stops automatically and saves the file

Activity What to do
Walking Walk at a normal pace, continuously and in a straight line or loop
Running Run at a moderate pace — clearly different from walking
Jumping Jump in place repeatedly, approximately once per second

Tips for clean data: - Keep the phone in your hand — do not put it in your pocket - Start moving before the countdown begins, not after - Stay consistent throughout the 20 seconds

Step 4 — Confirmation After each recording the script prints the filename, sample count, and recording duration. A typical result:

Saved: walk_jane_smith_a03.mat  |  2014 samples  |  20.1 s  |  100 Hz

If you see WARNING: No data received — check your phone connection and re-run the script.

After all three recordings the script prints a summary:

=== Ready to submit ===
Attach these three files to the Canvas assignment:
  walk_jane_smith_a03.mat
  run_jane_smith_a03.mat
  jump_jane_smith_a03.mat

Submitting Your Files

  1. Go to Canvas → Assignments → L11 Lab Sensor Data
  2. Click Submit Assignment
  3. Click Choose Files and select all three .mat files listed in the script summary above
  4. Click Submit
  5. Confirm Canvas shows “Submitted” — take a screenshot if you like

Before you leave lab: verify that Canvas shows Submitted and that all three filenames are listed in your submission. If you have any trouble, email the three files directly to your TA before midnight.

What Happens Next

  • Lecture 12 (Thursday): you will design a results structure and extend the batch loop using synthetic data — the same format as the files you collected today.
  • All labs conclude Monday: once every section has submitted, your TA will post class_files.m in the course chat. Running it defines the files variable containing the full class dataset.
  • In the lab the following week: paste or run class_files.m — your batch analysis loop processes all 164 students’ recordings without any other changes to the loop body.
