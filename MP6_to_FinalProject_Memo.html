MEMO: How MiniProject 6 Prepares You for the Final Project

MP6 is more than an activity classifier lab. It is your first end-to-end run of the engineering loop that both final-project tracks depend on: analyze data, make a decision, act on it.

The Pattern You Built in MP6

You took raw sensor files and reduced them to actions:

  1. Extract a feature. Peak resultant acceleration collapsed a thousand samples to one number.
  2. Derive a decision boundary from labeled data. Rather than guess a threshold, you let the class dataset show where walk ends and run begins.
  3. Classify and measure. Each recording got a predicted label, and per-class accuracy revealed where the boundary held and where it broke.

Part 4 asked the question that matters most in engineering: where does my classifier fail, and what feature would fix it? Bring that habit forward.

Track A (Kemper Tracker)

Your app must decide, in real time, whether the user is walking flat, climbing stairs, or riding an elevator, then act by updating the floor counter, switching regimes, or refreshing the indoor position.

  • Feature: vertical-axis peaks, peak rate, and sustained offsets (Appendix A), not peak alone.
  • Boundary: still derived from labeled data. Record short labeled clips of stairs and elevator rides, then set thresholds the MP6 way; do not guess.
  • Action: what MP6 stopped short of, namely changing app state (floor counter, regime indicator, displayed map) when the decision fires.

Track B (Networked Sensor Game)

Your GestureDetector class is a real-time, two-class classifier: gesture, or no gesture.

  • Feature: resultant acceleration on a moving window, smoothed with movmean.
  • Boundary: your shake threshold. Tune it the MP6 way. Record deliberate shakes and normal handling, look at the peak distributions, set the threshold where the two groups separate. The Personal Gesture Calibration reach feature is literally MP6 Part 4 applied per player.
  • Action: publish the move to ThingSpeak so the other player's app updates.

What MP6 Did Not Cover (Thursday's Lecture)

MP6 closes the loop locally: the script prints to your screen. Both projects close the loop across the network. Thursday introduces ThingSpeak, the missing piece between decide and act when the action lands on another machine.

One Specific Habit to Carry Forward

Per-class accuracy. Overall accuracy hides failure modes. When you tune your stair detector or your shake detector, count false positives and false negatives separately. That habit is the difference between a project that demos well and one that breaks the first time someone uses it slightly differently than you expected.
